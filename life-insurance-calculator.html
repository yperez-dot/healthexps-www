Have you used any tobacco or nicotine in the last 12 months?
Cigarettes, cigars, vaping, chew, patches, or gum.
No
✔

Answer No only if

No means no tobacco or nicotine in the last 12 months — including vapes, nicotine gum, and patches. Carriers verify this during underwriting, so an accurate answer here avoids a surprise when the real offer comes back.

Yes — cigarettes
✔

What this means

Any cigarette use in the last 12 months counts, including occasional or social smoking. Every carrier rates cigarettes as tobacco, so this is the one there’s no variation on.

Yes — cigars or pipe
✔

This one varies by carrier

Occasional cigar or pipe use is treated differently across carriers — some will still consider non-tobacco rates for a handful of cigars a year. Answer honestly and your advisor will check which carriers apply that.

Yes — vape / e-cigarette
✔

Vaping usually counts

Most carriers rate vaping the same as cigarettes, even when the liquid has no nicotine in it. A few treat it more favorably — worth asking your advisor rather than assuming either way.

Yes — chewing tobacco
✔

What this includes

Chewing tobacco, snuff, and dip all count here. Some carriers rate smokeless tobacco more favorably than cigarettes, so this is worth flagging to your advisor.

?Why We Ask — About Tobacco▼

Tobacco use significantly affects life insurance rates — often 2–3× higher. If you’ve been tobacco-free for 12 months or more, you may qualify for non-smoker pricing.

In the last 5 years, have you been treated for any of these?
Check all that apply. Being honest gets you an accurate number.
Heart disease, heart attack, or stroke
✔

Why this matters

This includes a diagnosed heart condition, a past heart attack, or a stroke — not just risk factors like family history. If you're unsure, select it; your agent can clarify during the call.

Cancer (other than basal cell skin cancer)
✔

Why this matters

Excludes basal cell skin cancer, which most carriers don't count. Other skin cancers and all internal cancers apply. If you had cancer more than 5 years ago, select "None of these."

Diabetes
✔

Why this matters

Applies to Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes. Well-controlled diabetes with medication often still qualifies for standard rates — selecting this doesn't disqualify you.

COPD, emphysema, or sleep apnea
✔

Why this matters

COPD and emphysema are chronic lung conditions; sleep apnea counts if diagnosed and treated. Mild sleep apnea well-managed with a CPAP often qualifies for preferred rates.

Kidney or liver disease
✔

Why this matters

Includes chronic kidney disease (CKD), kidney failure, cirrhosis, hepatitis C, or other diagnosed liver conditions. Single kidney stones typically don't count.

High blood pressure or cholesterol, controlled with medication
✔

Why this matters

Controlled BP or cholesterol on medication is very common and usually still qualifies for standard or preferred rates. Select this only if you're currently taking medication for it.

None of these✔
