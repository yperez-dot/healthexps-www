What Is a Special Enrollment Period for Medicare in Florida? (2026 Guide)

Published April 9, 2026

If you've ever missed the Annual Enrollment Period (AEP) — which runs from October 15 through December 7 each year — you may assume you're stuck with your current Medicare coverage until next fall. But that's not always the case. A Special Enrollment Period (SEP) is a federally protected window that allows Medicare-eligible individuals to enroll in, switch, or drop a Medicare plan outside of the standard Annual Enrollment Period. If a qualifying life event applies to you, you may be able to make changes to your coverage right now.

This 2026 guide explains what an SEP is, which events trigger one, how long it lasts, and what's unique for Florida beneficiaries — including a disaster-related SEP still in effect following Hurricane Milton.

What Is a Special Enrollment Period (SEP)?

A Special Enrollment Period is a time-limited window created by the federal government that gives Medicare beneficiaries the right to change their coverage when certain life events occur. These changes can include enrolling in a Medicare Advantage plan, switching from one Medicare Advantage plan to another, returning to Original Medicare (Parts A and B), or joining or dropping a Part D prescription drug plan. Not sure which type of plan to choose? See our Medicare Advantage vs Original Medicare comparison.

The SEP exists because life doesn't always follow the AEP calendar. People move, lose jobs, gain new benefits, or face emergencies — and Medicare is designed to accommodate those realities.

Common Qualifying Events That Trigger a Medicare SEP

Not every life change qualifies. The following are the most common federally recognized events that open a Special Enrollment Period for Medicare beneficiaries:

  • Moving to a new service area: If you permanently relocate to a county or ZIP code not covered by your current Medicare Advantage or Part D plan, you qualify for an SEP to choose a plan available in your new area.
  • Losing employer or union coverage: If you or a covered family member loses group health insurance through an employer or union, this triggers an SEP. This commonly affects people who retire or whose employer changes benefit offerings.
  • Your plan leaves the market: If your current Medicare Advantage or Part D plan loses its contract with Medicare or stops operating in your area, you receive an SEP to select a new plan.
  • Gaining Medicaid eligibility: If you become eligible for Medicaid (Florida Medicaid) or receive a Low Income Subsidy (LIS/Extra Help) for Part D costs, you may qualify for a special enrollment window. Dual-eligible beneficiaries — those enrolled in both Medicare and Medicaid — have even more flexibility (see below).
  • Returning from incarceration: Individuals released from incarceration after serving a sentence may use a special enrollment window to join Medicare Advantage or a Part D plan.
  • PACE program enrollment or disenrollment: The Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly (PACE) provides comprehensive care for qualifying seniors. Joining or leaving PACE triggers a coverage change SEP.
  • Enrollment in a 5-Star plan: Once per year (December 8 through November 30), beneficiaries may switch to a Medicare Advantage or Part D plan that has earned a 5-Star quality rating from Medicare.

How Long Does an SEP Last?

In most cases, an SEP lasts two months from the date of the qualifying event. For example, if you lose employer coverage on August 1, you generally have until September 30 to enroll in a new plan. Missing this window typically means waiting until the next AEP.

Timing matters. Some SEPs begin before the event (such as moving) while others begin on the date the event occurs (such as losing coverage). It is critical to act promptly once you know a qualifying event has happened or is approaching.

Documentation May Be Required (DST-SEPs)

For certain Special Enrollment Periods — sometimes referred to as Documented Special Enrollment Periods (DST-SEPs) — Medicare or your plan may require you to submit supporting documentation to verify that the qualifying event took place. Examples include proof of prior coverage (an insurance termination letter), proof of a move (a new utility bill or lease agreement), or proof of Medicaid eligibility. Gathering these documents before you begin your enrollment application can prevent delays.

Dual-Eligible Beneficiaries Can Switch Anytime

If you are dual eligible — meaning you are enrolled in both Medicare and Florida Medicaid — you have significantly more flexibility than most Medicare beneficiaries. Dual-eligible individuals can switch Medicare Advantage plans once per calendar quarter during the first three quarters of the year (January–March, April–June, and July–September), and they are not restricted to specific qualifying events. This ongoing flexibility ensures that dual-eligible enrollees can always access a plan that works with their Medicaid benefits.

Florida-Specific SEP: Hurricane Milton Disaster Relief (Ends July 31, 2026)

Florida residents affected by Hurricane Milton may still be eligible for a disaster-related Special Enrollment Period. This federally declared disaster SEP allows impacted beneficiaries in designated counties to make changes to their Medicare Advantage and Part D coverage outside of standard enrollment windows.

Important: The Hurricane Milton disaster SEP is scheduled to end on July 31, 2026. If you were displaced, lost access to your providers, or experienced disruptions to your coverage due to the hurricane and have not yet taken advantage of this SEP, time is running short. What Can You Change During an SEP?

Depending on the specific type of SEP, you may be able to:

  • Enroll in a Medicare Advantage (Part C) plan for the first time
  • Switch from one Medicare Advantage plan to a different Medicare Advantage plan
  • Drop a Medicare Advantage plan and return to Original Medicare (Parts A and B)
  • Join, switch, or drop a Medicare Part D prescription drug plan
  • Add a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policy — note that Medigap has its own enrollment rules and medical underwriting may apply outside your initial enrollment period

Not every SEP allows every type of change. The specific changes available depend on which qualifying event triggered your SEP.

What an SEP Does NOT Do

An SEP does not override all Medicare enrollment rules. If you did not enroll in Medicare Part B when first eligible and did not have qualifying coverage (such as employer insurance), a late enrollment penalty and a separate General Enrollment Period apply. An SEP is specifically for people who are already on Medicare and experience a life change — it is not a remedy for initially missing Medicare enrollment deadlines.

How to Use Your SEP in Florida

If you believe you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period, here are the recommended steps:

  1. Confirm your qualifying event. Make sure your situation fits one of the federally recognized SEP triggers described above.
  2. Check your timing. Verify when your SEP window opened and when it closes — most are just 60 days.
  3. Gather your documentation. Collect any proof of the qualifying event (termination letters, relocation documents, Medicaid award letters, etc.).
  4. Compare plans available in your area. Florida is one of the most competitive Medicare markets in the country, with dozens of plans available across Miami-Dade, Broward, and surrounding counties.
  5. Work with a licensed independent Medicare broker. An independent broker can compare every plan in your area at no cost to you — and they get paid the same commission regardless of which plan you choose.
