## Are you self-employed or a small employer?

HealthCare.gov says you're **self-employed** if you have a business that takes in income but has no employees. That includes freelancers, consultants, independent contractors and gig workers ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/self-employed/)).

- **No employees:** use the individual Marketplace.
- **At least one employee** other than yourself, a spouse, a family member or an owner: you may be able to use the **SHOP Marketplace** for small businesses to cover yourself and your employees.
- **Only independent contractors?** Hiring contractors doesn't make you an employer. Employees are generally workers you report on a W-2.

## Your options: Marketplace vs. private plans

| | Marketplace (HealthCare.gov) | Off-exchange ACA plan | Non-ACA plans (e.g. short-term) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subsidy (premium tax credit) | Yes, if you qualify | No | No |
| Covers pre-existing conditions | Yes | Yes | Not required by federal rules |
| When you can enroll | Open Enrollment or a Special Enrollment Period | Generally the same enrollment periods as Marketplace plans | Varies by plan |

**The Marketplace** is where you get the premium tax credit and, on Silver plans, extra savings on deductibles and copays if your income qualifies ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/choose-a-plan/plans-categories/)).

**Private, off-exchange ACA plans** are sold directly by insurers. They follow the same ACA rules but come with no subsidy. They can make sense if your income is above the subsidy limit, or if a plan with your doctors is sold only off-exchange.

**Non-ACA plans**, such as short-term coverage, aren't subject to federal rules that ban pre-existing condition exclusions or require comprehensive coverage ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/short-term-limited-duration-insurance-and-independent-noncoordinated-excepted-benefits-coverage-cms)). We explain the trade-offs before you buy anything. See our page on [private health insurance in Miami](/private-health-insurance-miami/) for more.

## How to estimate your income for a subsidy

The Marketplace asks for your **net self-employment income**, sometimes called profit: income minus business expenses. It's the amount you report on Schedule C, and you report a loss if expenses are higher ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/self-employed/income/)).

1. **Start with last year's net profit** from your tax return.
2. **Adjust it** for what you realistically expect in 2027, such as new clients, lost contracts or rate changes.
3. **Add other household income**, including a spouse's wages. Count everyone you'll claim on your taxes.
4. **Update your application during the year** if your income changes.

**Why accuracy matters:** if you earn more than you reported, you could have to pay back some or all of the premium tax credit at tax time. If you earn less, you could qualify for more savings ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/self-employed/income/)). You may also be asked to upload a self-employment ledger, a simple record of income and expenses, to confirm your income.

### 2027 subsidy income range

For 2027 coverage, the premium tax credit is generally available with household income between **100% and 400% of the federal poverty level** ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/federal-poverty-level-fpl/)), using the 2026 [HHS poverty guidelines](https://aspe.hhs.gov/topics/poverty-economic-mobility/poverty-guidelines):

| Household size | 100% FPL | 400% FPL |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $15,960 | $63,840 |
| 2 | $21,640 | $86,560 |
| 3 | $27,320 | $109,280 |
| 4 | $33,000 | $132,000 |

Above 400%, there's no premium tax credit for 2027. To see what plans actually cost in Miami-Dade and Broward with and without a subsidy, read [how much health insurance costs in Miami](/blog/how-much-does-health-insurance-cost-miami/).

**Married?** In most cases you must file a joint return to get the premium tax credit. And if your spouse's job offers a plan that covers spouses and dependents, you usually won't qualify for savings ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/self-employed/)).

## The self-employed health insurance deduction

The IRS lets many self-employed people deduct what they pay for health insurance. From the [Instructions for Form 7206](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7206) (2025 tax year):

- **Who can claim it:** generally, you were self-employed with a **net profit** on Schedule C or F, a partner with self-employment earnings, or a more-than-2% S corporation shareholder with wages from the company.
- **What it covers:** medical, dental and vision insurance, and qualified long-term care insurance, for you, your spouse and your dependents. It can also cover a child under 27, even if not your dependent.
- **What it doesn't cover:** any month you were eligible for a health plan subsidized by your employer or your spouse's employer.
- **Limits:** it's tied to the net earnings of the business the plan is under, and it doesn't lower your self-employment tax.
- **Where it goes:** Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 17, figured on [Form 7206](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-7206) or the worksheet in the Form 1040 instructions.
- **Also getting the premium tax credit?** The two are figured together. [IRS Publication 974](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p974) explains how.

We're insurance brokers, not tax advisors. Confirm the deduction with your tax professional.

## Open Enrollment vs. Special Enrollment

**Open Enrollment for 2027** ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/quick-guide/dates-and-deadlines/)):

- **November 1, 2026:** enrollment opens.
- **December 15, 2026:** enroll by this date for coverage starting January 1, 2027.
- **January 15, 2027:** last day. Plans picked December 16 to January 15 start February 1.

**Outside Open Enrollment**, you need a Special Enrollment Period, usually within 60 days of a life event ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage-outside-open-enrollment/special-enrollment-period/)):

- **Leaving a job to work for yourself?** Losing job-based coverage, including COBRA when it runs out, qualifies you. Voluntarily dropping COBRA does not. Compare [COBRA vs. the Marketplace](/blog/cobra-vs-marketplace-losing-job-coverage/) and our [COBRA alternatives in Miami](/cobra-alternatives-miami/).
- **Moving** to a new ZIP code or county, **getting married**, or **having or adopting a baby**.
- **Losing Medicaid or CHIP.**

If you later take a job that offers health coverage, you can cancel your Marketplace plan. In most cases, an offer of job-based coverage ends your eligibility for the premium tax credit ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/self-employed/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get health insurance if I'm self-employed with no employees?

Yes. If your business has no employees, you're considered self-employed and you buy coverage in the individual Health Insurance Marketplace, the same as any individual ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/self-employed/)). You can also buy an ACA plan directly from an insurer, without a subsidy.

### What income do I put on my Marketplace application?

Your best estimate of your **net** self-employment income for 2027, meaning profit after business expenses, plus any other household income. Net self-employment income is what you report on Schedule C ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/self-employed/income/)).

### What happens if my income ends up higher than I estimated?

You could have to pay back some or all of the premium tax credit you received when you file your taxes. If it ends up lower, you could qualify for more. Update your application as soon as your expected income changes ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/self-employed/income/)).

### Can I deduct my health insurance premiums?

Often, yes. The IRS self-employed health insurance deduction can cover premiums for you, your spouse and dependents if you had a net profit and weren't eligible for an employer-subsidized plan that month. You figure it with [Form 7206](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-7206) or the Form 1040 worksheet. Ask your tax professional how it applies to you.

### Can I sign up outside Open Enrollment?

Only with a Special Enrollment Period, such as losing job-based coverage, moving to a new ZIP code or county, getting married or having a baby ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage-outside-open-enrollment/special-enrollment-period/)). Open Enrollment for 2027 runs November 1, 2026, to January 15, 2027.

### I have one employee. Do I still use the individual Marketplace?

If your business has at least one employee other than yourself, a spouse, a family member or an owner, you may be able to use the SHOP Marketplace for small businesses instead ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/self-employed/)). Hiring independent contractors alone doesn't make you an employer.
