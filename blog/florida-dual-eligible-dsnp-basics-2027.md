# Florida Dual-Eligible D-SNP Basics for 2027

**If you have both Medicare and Medicaid, you may be able to join a Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan (D-SNP), a type of Medicare Advantage plan built for people with both kinds of coverage.**

Having two programs can be confusing: different cards, different rules, different phone numbers. D-SNPs are designed to help coordinate your Medicare and Medicaid benefits. Here's how they work in plain language, and what's changing for 2027.

<div class="alert-box"><strong>You may have more chances to enroll than most people</strong><span>Medicare gives people with Medicaid or Extra Help special enrollment opportunities during the year, not only during Open Enrollment (October 15 – December 7, 2026).</span></div>

## What is a D-SNP?

A D-SNP is a Medicare Advantage plan that only enrolls people who are entitled to both Medicare and Medicaid. Like all Special Needs Plans, D-SNPs provide care coordination and tailor their benefits, provider choices and drug lists to the people they serve. Every D-SNP must have a contract with the state Medicaid agency.

## Who may qualify?

According to CMS, D-SNP Medicaid eligibility categories can include:

| Medicaid category | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Full Medicaid | Full Medicaid benefits. |
| QMB (Qualified Medicare Beneficiary), with or without other Medicaid | A Medicare Savings Program that helps pay Medicare premiums and cost sharing. |
| SLMB (Specified Low-Income Medicare Beneficiary), with or without other Medicaid | A Medicare Savings Program that helps pay the Part B premium. |
| QI (Qualifying Individual) and QDWI (Qualified Disabled and Working Individual) | Other Medicare Savings Program categories. |

Each D-SNP decides which of these groups it enrolls, and states can define categories differently, so eligibility depends on the plan and on your Florida Medicaid status. Learn more in our guide to the [Medicare Savings Program](/blog/medicare-savings-program-lower-costs/).

## Your enrollment chances during the year

| If you... | Medicare says you can... | When |
|---|---|---|
| Have Medicaid or get Extra Help | Switch to a different Medicare drug plan, or drop a Medicare Advantage Plan with drug coverage and return to Original Medicare with a stand-alone drug plan | Once per calendar month; the change starts the first day of the next month |
| Have full Medicaid benefits | Join or switch to an integrated D-SNP, if one is available in your area | Once per calendar month; the change starts the first day of the next month |
| Are no longer eligible for Medicaid | Join, switch or drop a Medicare Advantage Plan with drug coverage or a drug plan | 3 full months from when you lose eligibility or are notified, whichever is later |

Not every D-SNP is integrated. An integrated D-SNP coordinates more closely with your Medicaid managed care plan. Ask the plan, or call 1-800-MEDICARE for a list of integrated D-SNPs.

## What changes for D-SNPs starting in 2027

CMS finalized rules to help more dual-eligible people get their Medicare and Medicaid from the same organization. According to CMS, **starting in 2027**, when a company offers a D-SNP and also runs a Medicaid managed care plan for full-benefit dual-eligible people in the same area, that D-SNP can only **newly** enroll people who are already in, or are enrolling in, the same company's Medicaid plan. Members who were already enrolled and aren't aligned can stay until 2030.

<div class="flag-note">How this affects a specific Florida plan depends on the plan, its Medicaid contract and your service area. Confirm with the plan, or let a licensed agent check for you.</div>

1. **Confirm your Medicaid status** (full Medicaid, QMB, SLMB or other).
2. **Check that your doctors and pharmacy are in the D-SNP's network.**
3. **Check your drugs** on the plan's drug list.
4. **Ask whether the plan is integrated** with your Medicaid plan.
5. **If you're in treatment,** Medicare says a new plan must give you at least 90 days before asking for a new prior approval for ongoing treatment. Check with the plan for details.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a D-SNP?

A Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan is a Medicare Advantage plan that only enrolls people who have both Medicare and Medicaid. It helps coordinate your Medicare and Medicaid benefits.

### Can I change plans outside Open Enrollment if I have Medicaid?

Often, yes. Medicare says people with Medicaid or Extra Help can change their drug coverage once a calendar month, and people with full Medicaid can join or switch to an integrated D-SNP once a month if one is available.

### What happens if I lose Medicaid?

Medicare gives you a Special Enrollment Period that lasts 3 full months from when you're no longer eligible or when you're notified, whichever is later. If you're in a D-SNP and no longer qualify, you may also have a Special Enrollment Period to join another plan.

### What changes in 2027?

According to CMS, starting in 2027, D-SNPs whose company also runs a Medicaid managed care plan in the same area can only newly enroll people who are in, or are enrolling in, that company's Medicaid plan. Existing unaligned members can stay until 2030.
