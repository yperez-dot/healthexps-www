# ACA Health Insurance in Miami: Your 2027 Guide to Plans, Costs & Subsidies

If you don't have health insurance through a job or Medicare, the ACA Marketplace — also called the Health Insurance Marketplace or Exchange — is where most Miami-Dade and Broward residents can find affordable coverage. Subsidies are still available for 2027 coverage, but the extra pandemic-era savings ended on December 31, 2025 ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/keep-or-change-plan/)), so check your own savings before you pick a plan.

Here's what you actually need to know.

## What Is the ACA Marketplace and Who Can Use It?

The ACA Marketplace is a government-run system where private insurance companies offer plans that meet federal coverage standards. In Florida, the federal marketplace at **healthcare.gov** is where you shop.

You can use it if you:
- Are a U.S. citizen or lawfully present resident
- Live in Florida
- Are not incarcerated
- Are not eligible for Medicare (if you're 65+, Medicare is usually your route)
- Don't have access to affordable job-based coverage that meets minimum standards

There's no income floor to enroll — but your income affects whether you qualify for subsidies that reduce your monthly premium.

## How Do Subsidies Work for 2027 Coverage?

The federal government offers two types of financial help:

**1. Premium Tax Credits (PTCs)** — reduce your monthly premium. For 2027 coverage, these credits are available to households earning between 100% and 400% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/federal-poverty-level-fpl/)). Savings for 2027 plans use the 2026 poverty guidelines, so the 400% cutoff is $63,840/year for one person, $86,560 for two, $109,280 for three, and $132,000 for a family of four ([HHS](https://aspe.hhs.gov/topics/poverty-economic-mobility/poverty-guidelines)).

**2. Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSRs)** — lower your deductibles and out-of-pocket costs. These only apply if you choose a Silver plan and your income is between 100% and 250% FPL ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/aptc-cost-sharing-reductions-overview.pdf)). For 2027 coverage, that's up to $39,900/year for a single person.

One important change: the extra pandemic-era savings ended on December 31, 2025 ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/keep-or-change-plan/)). Many people now get less help, and some no longer qualify. The standard premium tax credit is still available, so update your income on your application to see your real savings ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/keep-or-change-plan/)).

## What Plans Are Available in Miami-Dade and Broward?

ACA plans in South Florida are organized into four metal tiers:

- **Bronze** — lowest monthly premium, higher out-of-pocket costs
- **Silver** — mid-range premium, eligible for cost-sharing reductions
- **Gold** — higher premium, lower out-of-pocket costs when you use care
- **Platinum** — highest premium, lowest cost-sharing

Most carriers in the South Florida marketplace offer HMO plans — meaning you'll select a primary care doctor and need referrals for specialists. Network size varies significantly by carrier, which matters if you have established doctors.

## When Can You Enroll?

The main enrollment window — **Open Enrollment** — runs from **November 1 through January 15** each year ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/quick-guide/dates-and-deadlines/)). For 2027 coverage, pick a plan by December 15 and it starts January 1. Pick one December 16 to January 15 and it starts February 1.

Outside that window, you can still enroll if you have a **Special Enrollment Period (SEP)** triggered by a qualifying life event:

- Losing job-based coverage
- Moving to a new area
- Getting married or divorced
- Having a baby or adopting
- Losing Medicaid or CHIP coverage
- Turning 26 and aging off a parent's plan

You generally have **60 days** from the qualifying event to enroll, or 90 days if you lost Medicaid or CHIP ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage-outside-open-enrollment/special-enrollment-period/)). Missing that window means waiting until November.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Not enrolling because you assume it's too expensive.** The extra pandemic-era savings ended on December 31, 2025, but the standard premium tax credit still lowers premiums for many households. Check your real price before you decide.
2. **Picking the cheapest plan without checking the network.** Low-premium Bronze plans often have narrow networks — make sure your doctors and preferred hospitals are included.
3. **Forgetting to report income changes mid-year.** If your income changes significantly, update your application. If you get more tax credit than you qualify for, you'll have to pay back the difference at tax time ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/keep-or-change-plan/)).
4. **Missing the SEP window.** You have 60 days from a qualifying event. Don't wait.
