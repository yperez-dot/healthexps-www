# COBRA vs ACA Marketplace in Miami: Which One Actually Saves You More Money?

**Your COBRA letter shows the full price of your old job plan. Before you sign it, compare it with what a Marketplace plan would cost you in Miami-Dade or Broward for 2027. For many people who qualify for a subsidy, the difference is hundreds of dollars a month.**

<p class="source-note">Updated October 26, 2026, with KFF's 2026 employer premium survey and Florida's 2027 Marketplace estimates.</p>

<div class="quick-answer"><span class="qa-label">Quick answer</span>
<p>COBRA lets you keep your job plan, but you can be charged <strong>up to 102% of the plan's full cost</strong>. Based on KFF's 2026 average employer premiums, that's about <strong>$833 a month for one person</strong> and <strong>$2,406 a month for a family</strong>.</p>
<p>In Florida's 2027 examples, the average Silver Marketplace plan after the average subsidy is <strong>$381 a month for a single adult in Miami-Dade</strong> ($312 in Broward) and <strong>$1,219 for a family of four</strong> ($959 in Broward). That's about <strong>$450 to $520 a month less for one person</strong> and <strong>$1,190 to $1,450 a month less for a family</strong>.</p>
<p>No subsidy? The gap can shrink or flip. You have <strong>60 days</strong> after losing job coverage to choose either one.</p></div>

## Why COBRA costs so much

While you're working, your employer usually pays most of the premium. In KFF's 2026 survey, workers paid on average **18% of the premium for single coverage and 27% for family coverage**: about **$1,672 a year ($139 a month)** for single coverage and **$7,471 a year ($623 a month)** for family coverage ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2026-employer-health-benefits-survey/)).

With COBRA, your employer usually stops paying its share. Federal law lets the plan charge you **up to 102% of the full premium**: the whole cost plus 2% ([29 U.S.C. § 1162](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1162)). On an average plan, that turns about $139 a month into about $833.

## Why the Marketplace can cost less

The Marketplace has a tax credit that COBRA doesn't. For 2027, you generally qualify if your household income is **between 100% and 400% of the federal poverty level** and you don't have an affordable job-based offer ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/federal-poverty-level-fpl/)). Based on the [2026 HHS poverty guidelines](https://aspe.hhs.gov/topics/poverty-economic-mobility/poverty-guidelines), 400% is about:

- **$63,840** for one person
- **$132,000** for a family of four

The credit is based on your **expected income for the year you need coverage**, not last year's salary. If you lost your job partway through the year, your income may be lower than you think. For 2027, the IRS sets what you're expected to pay for the benchmark Silver plan at **2.15% to 10.22% of income** ([IRS Rev. Proc. 2026-26](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-26-26.pdf)).

### If you earn more than the cliff

Above 400% of the poverty level, there's no tax credit for 2027, so you pay the full Marketplace price. That's where the comparison gets close:

- In Florida's examples, the **unsubsidized** Silver price for a 28-year-old in Miami-Dade is $776, close to the $833 average COBRA price.
- For the family example, the unsubsidized Silver price is **$2,917 in Miami-Dade and $2,717 in Broward. That's more than the $2,406 average COBRA price.**
- Age matters. Marketplace premiums can be up to **3 times higher for older adults** than for younger ones ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/how-plans-set-your-premiums/)).

So if your income is above the cliff, compare your actual COBRA price with Bronze, Silver and Gold quotes before you decide. Sometimes COBRA is the better deal.

**Good to know**

- **Have a health condition or ongoing care?** An ACA plan's coverage rules may matter more to you than the price. You can also buy an ACA plan directly from an insurer. It keeps every ACA rule, but there's no tax credit.

## When COBRA still makes sense

COBRA's big advantage is that **it's the same plan**: same network, same doctors, same drug list, and what you've already paid toward this year's deductible still counts. A new plan starts a new deductible. COBRA may be the better choice if:

- **You're in treatment** and need to keep the same doctors or hospital.
- **You've already met most of your deductible** and have care coming up this year.
- **You'll have new job coverage soon** and only need a short bridge.
- **Your income is above the subsidy cliff**, and COBRA costs less than your unsubsidized quotes.
- **Your former employer pays part of COBRA** as part of severance. Use that lower price in your comparison.

**Turning 65 or already 65?** COBRA doesn't count as job-based coverage for Medicare. Getting COBRA doesn't extend your window to sign up for Part B without a penalty ([Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-does-medicare-coverage-start)). Talk to a [licensed Medicare agent](/medicare-agent-miami/) before you choose COBRA.

## Deadlines that matter

| Deadline | COBRA | Marketplace |
|---|---|---|
| Time to decide | At least **60 days** to elect ([29 U.S.C. § 1165](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1165)) | **60 days before or after** you lose job coverage ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage-outside-open-enrollment/special-enrollment-period/)) |
| First payment | Can't be required until **45 days** after you elect ([29 U.S.C. § 1162](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1162)) | Follow the due date your new insurer gives you |
| How long it lasts | Generally **18 months** after a job loss or cut in hours. Longer in some cases, such as a disability extension, where the plan can charge up to 150% ([26 CFR 54.4980B-8](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-54/section-54.4980B-8)) | Renews each year. 2027 Open Enrollment is **Nov 1, 2026 to Jan 15, 2027** ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/quick-guide/dates-and-deadlines/)) |

**Switching later:** you can move from COBRA to a Marketplace plan during Open Enrollment, when your COBRA runs out, or within 60 days of losing your job coverage. If you **drop COBRA early on purpose** or stop paying, that **doesn't** open a Special Enrollment Period ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/cobra-coverage/)). Decide inside your 60-day window.

**Small employer?** Federal COBRA doesn't apply to plans of employers that normally had fewer than 20 employees ([29 U.S.C. § 1161](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1161)). Florida's state continuation law covers smaller employers, and there the premium can be up to **115%** ([Florida Statutes § 627.6692](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/627.6692)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does COBRA cost per month?

Up to 102% of the plan's full premium ([29 U.S.C. § 1162](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1162)). Based on KFF's 2026 average employer premiums of $9,804 for single coverage and $28,303 for family coverage, that's about $833 a month for one person and $2,406 for a family ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2026-employer-health-benefits-survey/)). Your COBRA notice shows your exact price.

### Is the Marketplace cheaper than COBRA in Miami?

Often, if you qualify for a subsidy. In Florida's 2027 examples, a single adult in Miami-Dade pays about $381 a month after the average subsidy, and a family of four pays about $1,219 ([Florida OIR](https://www.floir.gov/docs-sf/life-health-libraries/federal-health-insurance/2027-plan-year/indiv-aca-average-single-36k.pdf)). Without a subsidy, Marketplace prices can be close to or higher than COBRA, especially for older adults and families.

### How long do I have to choose COBRA or a Marketplace plan?

You get at least 60 days to elect COBRA ([29 U.S.C. § 1165](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1165)). Losing job coverage also gives you a Special Enrollment Period to buy a Marketplace plan from 60 days before to 60 days after the loss ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage-outside-open-enrollment/special-enrollment-period/)).

### Can I drop COBRA later and switch to a Marketplace plan?

Yes during Open Enrollment (November 1 to January 15) or when your COBRA runs out. Outside those times, voluntarily dropping COBRA or stopping payments doesn't qualify you for a Special Enrollment Period ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/cobra-coverage/)).

### Do I have to sign up for COBRA first?

No. You don't have to enroll in COBRA, and you can compare it with Marketplace plans before you decide ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/cobra-coverage/)). If you elect COBRA within your election period, coverage generally goes back to the day your job coverage ended ([26 CFR 54.4980B-6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-54/section-54.4980B-6)).

### Are private plans outside the Marketplace cheaper than COBRA?

They can be, especially if you're healthy. A private plan is priced on your age and health, and some accept applications any month. There's no premium tax credit ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/premium-tax-credit/)) and coverage varies by plan, so we compare each quote with your COBRA price and show you what it covers before you buy anything.

### Is private health insurance cheaper than ACA if I don't qualify for a subsidy?

It can be. Over the subsidy limit, you pay full price for any ACA plan, and that price is the same whether you're healthy or not. A private plan is priced on your age and health, so a healthy person may get a lower quote. Your health can also affect approval, and coverage varies by plan ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/medical-underwriting/)). We'll put both quotes side by side and show you what each one covers.

### Can I buy private health insurance outside Open Enrollment?

Yes, some private plans accept applications any month, so you don't have to wait for Open Enrollment. ACA plans need Open Enrollment (November 1 to January 15) or a Special Enrollment Period, such as losing job coverage ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/quick-guide/dates-and-deadlines/)).
