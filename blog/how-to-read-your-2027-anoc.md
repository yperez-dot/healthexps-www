# How to Read Your 2027 ANOC (Annual Notice of Change)

**If you have a Medicare Advantage or Medicare drug plan, your plan sends an Annual Notice of Change (ANOC) each fall. It explains what will be different in your coverage starting January 1, 2027.**

It's easy to set this letter aside, but it's one of the most useful pieces of mail you'll get all year. Ten or fifteen minutes with it can save you surprises at the doctor's office or the pharmacy counter next year.

<div class="alert-box"><strong>Didn't get your 2027 ANOC?</strong><span>Medicare says plans send it in September. If you haven't received it, check your plan's member website or call the number on your member ID card and ask for a copy.</span></div>

## What to check in your ANOC

| Section | What to look for | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly premium | Is it going up, down or staying the same? | You usually still pay your Part B premium on top of any plan premium. |
| Deductibles, copays and coinsurance | Primary care, specialists, hospital stays, emergency care | These are what you pay when you actually use care. |
| Yearly out-of-pocket maximum | The most you'd pay for covered medical services (Medicare Advantage) | Important if you expect surgery, a hospital stay or ongoing treatment. |
| Drug list (formulary) | Is each of your drugs still covered? Did its tier change? New prior authorization, step therapy or quantity limits? | A tier change can change what you pay at the pharmacy. |
| Pharmacies | Is your pharmacy still in network, and still "preferred"? | Preferred pharmacies often cost less. |
| Doctors and hospitals | Any changes to the plan's network | Out-of-network care can cost more, or not be covered in an HMO. |
| Extra benefits | Dental, vision, hearing and other benefits | These can be added, reduced or removed each year. |
| Service area | Is your county still served? | If the plan leaves your area, you'll need a new plan. |

## A 15-minute way to read it

<div class="checklist-box"><strong>Before you start</strong><ul><li>Your ANOC and your plan member ID card</li><li>Your medication list, with doses</li><li>The names of your doctors, specialists, hospital and pharmacy</li></ul></div>

1. **Start with the summary.** Most ANOCs open with a short summary of the most important changes.
2. **Highlight every change** to costs, drugs, pharmacies and doctors.
3. **Look up each medication** in the plan's 2027 drug list and note its tier and any new rules.
4. **Confirm your doctors** with their offices and in the plan's 2027 provider directory.
5. **Write down anything that worries you** and bring it to a plan review.

## Part D drug costs in 2027

CMS has announced that the Part D annual out-of-pocket threshold is **$2,400 in 2027**. After your out-of-pocket costs for covered Part D drugs reach that amount, you pay nothing more for covered Part D drugs for the rest of the year. Your ANOC and your plan's Evidence of Coverage explain how your own plan's deductible and copays work.

## If you don't like what you see

You're not stuck. During Medicare's Open Enrollment, **October 15 to December 7, 2026**, you can switch to a different Medicare Advantage or drug plan, or move between Medicare Advantage and Original Medicare. Changes start January 1, 2027. If you like your plan after reading the ANOC, you generally don't need to do anything.

Before you switch, compare total yearly cost, your drug list and your doctors side by side. A lower premium isn't always the lowest cost overall.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the ANOC?

The Annual Notice of Change is a notice your Medicare plan sends each fall. It lists changes in coverage, costs and more that take effect in January.

### When should I get my ANOC?

Medicare says plans send it in September. If you didn't get it, contact your plan.

### Do I get an ANOC if I have Original Medicare?

The ANOC comes from Medicare plans, like Medicare Advantage and Medicare drug plans. If you have Original Medicare with a separate drug plan, the drug plan sends one.

### Is the ANOC the same as the Evidence of Coverage?

No. The ANOC summarizes what's changing. The Evidence of Coverage is the full, detailed description of your plan's benefits and rules.
