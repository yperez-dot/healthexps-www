What COBRA actually costs

COBRA means paying 100% of your employer's premium, plus a 2% admin fee — typically far more than the alternatives.

Single person
100% of your employer's premium, plus 2% admin fee
Family coverage
100% of your employer's family premium, plus 2% admin fee
Your alternatives — at a fraction of the cost:
ACA Marketplace plans
May be significantly less, depending on income. Subsidies for 100–400% FPL.
Private health insurance
Often far less than COBRA. Best for those above the subsidy cutoff.

COBRA costs depend on your specific employer plan. ACA and private plan costs depend on your income, household size, and age. A licensed broker will run your actual numbers at no cost — no obligation to enroll.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to find a COBRA alternative? ＋
You have 60 days from losing coverage to enroll in an ACA Marketplace plan through a Special Enrollment Period — anywhere in Florida.
I'm over 65 and on COBRA — is that okay? ＋
Not always. COBRA isn't creditable coverage for Medicare Part B — staying on it instead of enrolling may accrue a permanent late penalty. Talk to a licensed broker immediately.
Is COBRA ever worth keeping? ＋
Yes — if you're in active treatment, pregnant, or need to keep a very specific specialist. Otherwise, alternatives are almost always cheaper.
