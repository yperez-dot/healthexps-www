What Is Medicare? Parts A, B, C & D Explained.

A plain-English guide to the federal health insurance program — who qualifies, what each part covers, and how to choose the right plan in Florida.

What is Medicare?

Medicare is the federal health insurance program created in 1965 to provide coverage for Americans 65 and older. It also covers certain younger people with qualifying disabilities and people with End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) or ALS (Lou Gehrig’s disease).

Medicare is administered by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), a federal agency. It is not the same as Medicaid, a separate income-based program. Many people confuse the two — you can learn about the Medicare Savings Program if you think you may qualify for extra help with costs.

Medicare vs. Medicaid — key difference
Medicare is based on age (65+) or disability — not income. Medicaid is based on income and financial need. Some people qualify for both — they’re called “dual eligibles” and often receive extra benefits.

Who qualifies for Medicare?

1
You are 65 or older and a U.S. citizen or permanent legal resident who has lived in the U.S. at least 5 years.
2
You are under 65 with a disability and have received SSDI for at least 24 months.
3
You have End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) — permanent kidney failure requiring dialysis or a transplant.
4
You’ve been diagnosed with ALS — coverage begins immediately upon receiving disability benefits.

Most people who’ve worked and paid Medicare taxes at least 10 years (40 quarters) get Part A at no premium cost. If you haven’t worked enough quarters, you can still buy into Medicare.

The four parts of Medicare

A
Hospital Insurance
Covers inpatient hospital stays, skilled nursing facility care, hospice, and some home health care.
$0 premium for most · $1,736 deductible per benefit period (2026)
B
Medical Insurance
Covers doctor visits, outpatient care, preventive services, and durable medical equipment.
$202.90/month standard · $283 annual deductible (2026)
C
Medicare Advantage
Private insurance alternative to Original Medicare that often includes dental, vision, hearing, and drug coverage.
Varies · Many $0 premium options in South Florida
D
Prescription Drug Coverage
Adds prescription drug coverage when you have Original Medicare (Parts A & B). Sold by private insurers.
Varies · $2,100 out-of-pocket cap in 2026

Original Medicare vs. Medicare Advantage

When you turn 65, you have a fundamental choice: stick with Original Medicare (Parts A + B, with or without a Supplement and Part D), or switch to a Medicare Advantage plan (Part C) from a private insurer.

Original Medicare (Parts A + B)
Any doctor or hospital that accepts Medicare — nationwide
No referrals needed to see specialists
No annual out-of-pocket maximum (unless you add Medigap)
Separate Part D plan needed for prescriptions
Best for frequent travelers or complex conditions
Medicare Advantage (Part C)
Often includes dental, vision, hearing & transportation
Many plans have $0 monthly premium
Annual out-of-pocket maximum protects you from large bills
Usually includes Part D drug coverage
Network-based — verify your doctors are in-network

When do I sign up?

Missing your enrollment window can mean permanent late penalties. Key periods to know:

Initial Enrollment Period (IEP): a 7-month window starting 3 months before your 65th birthday month.
Annual Enrollment Period (AEP): October 15 – December 7 each year, effective January 1.
Special Enrollment Period (SEP): triggered by life events like losing employer coverage or moving.

Medicare in South Florida

South Florida — especially Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties — has one of the highest concentrations of Medicare Advantage plans in the country. That means more options, but also more complexity.

Local factors that matter here: which hospitals and specialists are in-network (Baptist Health, Jackson Health, Cleveland Clinic Florida, etc.), Spanish-language support, and the availability of give-back and extra-benefits programs.
