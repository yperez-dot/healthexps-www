Leaving a job? Compare COBRA alternatives first

With COBRA you usually pay 100% of the premium, including the part your employer used to pay, plus a small administrative fee. Before you sign up, compare it with a Marketplace or private plan.

What is private health insurance?

Any plan you buy directly — rather than through an employer or Medicare — is "private" coverage. That includes ACA Marketplace plans (bought "on-exchange," with possible subsidies) and off-exchange plans sold directly by carriers. Both cover individuals and families; the right one depends on your income and timing.

What most people mean by “private”

Coverage bought directly from an insurer instead of through HealthCare.gov, with no Marketplace tax credit. ACA plans sold this way follow the same enrollment windows; some non-ACA plan types accept applications any month but may use medical underwriting.

ACA Marketplace (Obamacare)

Also individual coverage, but sold on-exchange. Income-based subsidies may apply. Enrollment is Open Enrollment or a Special Enrollment Period. Pre-existing conditions are generally covered.

Underwriting, honestly

Off-exchange private plans may use medical underwriting — a health questionnaire and a review of pre-existing conditions. Approval is not guaranteed. Some conditions can raise the premium, add a waiting period, or lead to a decline. That is a real difference from the Marketplace, not a scare line.

ACA Marketplace plans are generally guaranteed-issue: they cannot deny you for a pre-existing condition, and many households qualify for premium tax credits. If a subsidy would apply, we usually start there. If you need coverage outside Open Enrollment and do not have a qualifying event, off-exchange may be the path that is actually open.

We tell you which rules apply to your application before you apply — subsidy vs private — so you are not guessing.

Private vs ACA Marketplace, in plain terms

  • Tax credits: only available through the Marketplace, based on household income.
  • Pre-existing conditions: Marketplace plans cannot reject you or charge you more. Non-ACA plans may use medical underwriting.
  • Benefits: every Marketplace plan covers the same essential health benefits. Non-ACA plans may cover less, so we read the benefits with you.
  • When you can enroll: ACA plans, on or off the Marketplace, follow Open Enrollment (November 1 to January 15) or a Special Enrollment Period. Some non-ACA plans accept applications any month.

Sources: premium tax credit, pre-existing conditions, medical underwriting, dates and deadlines (HealthCare.gov).

What does health insurance cost in Miami?

There is no honest single number. For Marketplace plans, insurers can only use five things to set your premium:

Age

Premiums can be up to 3 times higher for older people than for younger ones.

Location

Your ZIP code and county change the price. Miami-Dade and Broward are priced separately.

Tobacco use

Insurers can charge tobacco users up to 50% more.

Individual or family

Covering a spouse or dependents costs more than covering one person.

Plan category

Bronze plans usually have lower premiums and higher costs when you get care. Platinum is the opposite.

Tax credit

If you qualify, a premium tax credit lowers what you pay each month. Non-ACA plans have no tax credit and may also price on your health history.

Source: HealthCare.gov, How plans set your premiums.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Common questions about private health insurance

What is private health insurance in Miami?+
It is health coverage you buy yourself instead of getting it through a job or Medicare. In Miami that can be an ACA Marketplace plan from HealthCare.gov, an ACA-compliant plan bought directly from an insurer, or a non-ACA plan type such as short-term coverage. We compare the options we can offer for your ZIP and explain the rules for each one.
Who is private health insurance a good fit for?+
It often fits people who are self-employed or 1099, small business owners, people between jobs, people shopping outside Open Enrollment, and households that do not qualify for a Marketplace premium tax credit. If you qualify for a tax credit, a Marketplace plan is usually the first one we price.
Can I buy health insurance outside Open Enrollment?+
Open Enrollment for Marketplace plans runs November 1 to January 15. Outside it, you need a Special Enrollment Period, for example after losing job-based coverage or moving to a new ZIP code or county, and you generally have 60 days to pick a plan. ACA-compliant plans sold outside the Marketplace follow the same windows. Some non-ACA plan types accept applications any month, but they may use medical underwriting and may not cover everything an ACA plan covers.
What is the difference between private insurance and an ACA Marketplace plan?+
Premium tax credits are only available through the Marketplace and are based on your household income. All Marketplace plans cover the same essential health benefits and cannot reject you or charge you more for a pre-existing condition. A non-ACA private plan has no tax credit and may review your health history. We put both side by side for your ZIP, doctors and budget.
Do private plans cover pre-existing conditions?+
Marketplace plans must cover treatment for pre-existing conditions from the day coverage starts. Non-ACA plans may use medical underwriting, which means the insurer can review your health to decide whether to offer coverage, at what price and with what exclusions. We tell you which rules apply before you apply.
I am leaving my job. What are my COBRA alternatives?+
With COBRA you usually pay 100% of the premium, including the part your employer used to pay, plus a small administrative fee. Losing job-based coverage opens a 60-day Special Enrollment Period for a Marketplace plan, so you can compare it with COBRA first. If you start COBRA and later drop it on your own outside Open Enrollment, that alone does not qualify you for a Special Enrollment Period.
Does it cost anything to use a broker?+
No. You do not pay us a fee to compare or enroll. Licensed agents and brokers are typically paid a commission by the insurance company. We are bilingual, based in Doral, and we quote the carriers we are appointed with. If another path fits you better, we tell you.
Can my small business get group health insurance any time of year?+
Generally yes. Businesses with 1 to 50 employees can start offering small business coverage any month of the year. If you are self-employed with no employees, you shop the individual market instead. We compare both paths for you and your team.
I am self-employed. Can I use an HSA?+
An HSA-eligible high-deductible health plan can fit if you want a lower monthly premium and a tax-advantaged account for medical costs. Whether HSA deposits or premiums are deductible depends on your taxes, so talk with your tax professional. This is not tax advice.

Yes. We check whether your doctors and hospitals are in-network for the plans we compare, including Baptist Health, Jackson Health System, UHealth / University of Miami, Memorial Healthcare System, Cleveland Clinic Florida and HCA Florida Healthcare. We are not in-network with these systems; we check each plan's directory because networks change every year.
