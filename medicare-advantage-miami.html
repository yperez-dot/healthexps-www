What is Medicare Advantage?

A private plan alternative to Original Medicare — often with $0 premium, dental, vision, and more.

Original Medicare vs. Medicare Advantage — simplified

Think of it this way: Original Medicare is like a basic phone plan — it covers the essentials (hospital and doctor visits) but not much else. Medicare Advantage (Part C) is like an all-in-one bundle — a private insurer takes over your Medicare coverage and often adds dental, vision, hearing, gym membership, and more, sometimes for $0 extra per month.

Original Medicare alone
  • Hospital coverage (Part A)
  • Doctor visits (Part B)
  • No dental or vision included
  • No annual out-of-pocket maximum
  • Works with any doctor nationwide
Medicare Advantage (Part C)
  • Everything Original Medicare covers
  • Often includes dental, vision, hearing
  • Annual out-of-pocket maximum built in
  • Usually network-based (HMO or PPO)
  • Often $0 monthly premium

Important: You must use the plan's network of doctors and hospitals. Always verify your doctors are in-network before enrolling. Our brokers do this for you — free.

Plan structures

HMO or PPO — which fits your lifestyle?

Medicare Advantage comes in several plan types. Understanding the difference helps you choose the right network structure for your doctors and lifestyle.

Most common in Miami
HMO (Health Maintenance Organization)
You choose a primary care doctor and get referrals to specialists within the plan's network. Generally lower premiums and copays. You must use in-network providers except in emergencies.
Best for: people who have local doctors in-network and want lower out-of-pocket costs
More flexibility
PPO (Preferred Provider Organization)
See any Medicare-accepting doctor — in or out of network. No referrals needed for specialists. Out-of-network costs are higher, but you have freedom to choose any provider.
Best for: people who travel frequently or want flexibility to see specialists without referrals
Dual eligible
D-SNP (Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan)
Designed for people with both Medicare and Medicaid. Often $0 premium with enhanced benefits including grocery cards, OTC allowances, transportation, and care coordination.
Best for: people who qualify for both Medicare and Medicaid
Chronic conditions
C-SNP (Chronic Condition Special Needs Plan)
Specialized plans for people with serious chronic conditions like diabetes, COPD, or heart failure. Includes condition-specific care teams and often the same extra benefits as D-SNPs.
Best for: people with confirmed chronic diagnoses who want specialized care coordination

Medicare Advantage (Part C) is an alternative to Original Medicare offered by private insurance companies approved by Medicare. It must cover everything Original Medicare covers, and usually adds benefits like dental, vision, hearing, and prescription drug coverage — often with a $0 monthly premium. The trade-off is that you must use the plan's network of doctors and hospitals (except in emergencies).
